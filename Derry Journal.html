Derry City humbled after second half horror show at Dalymount

By Simon Collins
Published 21st Aug 2026, 21:47 BST

Bohemians 5 Derry City 2

MARK Connolly must be scratching his head wondering what went wrong as Derry City capitulated during eight minutes of madness against Bohemians at Dalymount Park.

Heading into the half-time break Derry were seemingly in control after skipper Michael Duffy's eighth goal of the season on 34 minutes.

Whatever Alan Reynolds said at the interval certainly did the trick as the home side incredibly bounced back with three quickfire goals from Patrick Hickey, Connor Parsons and Douglas James-Taylor - all three from set-pieces!

To compound matters former Bohs favourite James Clarke saw red for a robust tackle on Connor Parsons, reducing Derry to 10 men.

Tyler Smith pulled one back from close range for Derry on 62 minutes to make it 3-2 but Bohemians turned the screw and a brace from Ross Tierney - his second a stunning strike from distance - ended a horrendous night in the capital for Derry.

When sub Darragh Burns went down inside the box in stoppage time under the challenge of Hickey, Duffy converted from the penalty spot.

It was Connolly's first defeat as interim boss, a chastening one that leaves Derry 14 points behind Bohemians.

It was the fourth and potentially final meeting between the teams this season unless of course they both progress and clash in the FAI Cup. It was also the last meeting at the old Dalymount Park ground until at least 2029 as renovations begin at the end of the year.

It was one win apiece in the previous three fixtures but Bohs will no doubt be smarting from the 4-1 hammering by Derry at Celtic Park - a result Alan Reynolds described as his team's worst performance of the season.

Mark Connolly made two changes from the team which defeated UCD in the cup as Chapman and Barr came in for Burns and Dummigan who both dropped to the bench.

Former Derry striker Colm Whelan, who scored twice in the 4-0 win over Longford Town last week, started on the bench. He was one of FIVE changes made by the Dubliners who started the game 12 points ahead of the Foylesiders.

Bohs dominated the opening stages but Derry broke dangerously on 13 minutes when Smith picked the pocket of Hickey inside the home side's final third. The striker played Duffy in behind and his cross was cleverly flicked towards goal at the near post by Clarke but beaten away by Walters.

Mullen skipped past O'Reilly wide on the left side before picking out James-Taylor on the six yard line but somehow the striker's first-time effort went wide of the post on 16 minutes.

Derry survived a quick counter attack just past the hour mark when McClean's loose pass sent Parsons scampering clear on the right. His cross across the face of goal was crucially cleared by Barr with James-Taylor lurking behind the Derry defender.

Straight down the other end Cotter raced 40 yards down the right flank and crossed towards Duffy who took a touch on the edge of the box and fired a right footed strike into the bottom corner to break the deadlock.

Harry Vaughan played Mullen in behind the Derry defence with a sublime clipped pass but his strike from 12 yards at full stretch crashed off the crossbar four minutes before the break.

Bohs made an emphatic start to the second half and within two minutes were back on level terms. Flores' inswinging corner kick was met by a towering header from Hickey.

Two minutes later Devoy's quick-thinking free-kick sent Parsons free inside the box and the winger found the bottom corner at Maher's near post to turn the game on its head.

It was a disastrous start for Derry and it got worse as they conceded a third goal in the space of seven minutes. From another Flores corner the ball was allowed to bounce inside the penalty area and James-Taylor fired past Maher.

Derry were down to 10 men on 55 minutes after Clarke's rash challenge from behind on Parsons as referee Rob Harvey flashed a straight red.

Maher made a crucial point-blank save at his near post from Ross Tierney as Derry were under siege.

Connolly made a triple substitution on 62 minutes, including Brandon Fleming who returned from a broken cheekbone injury and there was an immediate response.

Duffy crossed into a packed six yard box and O'Reilly got a touch at the back post before Smith rifled his first time strike into the net to reduce the deficit on 62 minutes.

Maher made another crucial save at his near post, turning Parsons' poked effort behind for a corner on 70 minutes.

Tierney did brilliantly to take the ball past Maher from a narrow angle before slotting into the net to put Bohs further ahead on 79 minutes.

And the midfielder put the final nail in the coffin for the visitors with two minutes to go as he rifled an unstoppable strike into the roof of the net.

Substitute Burns was adjudged to have been clipped by Hickey inside the penalty area in the 95th minute. It was a soft decision but Duffy fired coolly home from the spotkick to reduce the arrears.

It was a night to forget for Connolly and a Derry team who will now be looking over their shoulders again rather than looking at closing the gap on fourth spot.

Bohs: Walters: Byrne, Flores (McDonnell 82), Hickey, Mullen; Parsons (Strods 82), Vaughan (Myers 73) Tierney, Devoy, Power (Morahan 90); James-Taylor (Whelan 72).

Derry City: Maher: Cotter (Fleming 62), Barr, McClean, Lyons-Foster (Burns 84); Duffy, Chapman (Olayinka 62), Twisk (Dummigan 62), O'Reilly, Clarke; Smith.

Referee - Rob Harvey (Dublin)
